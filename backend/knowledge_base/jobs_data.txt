SECTION 1 — DATA ANALYST ROLES
==============================================================

TOP SKILLS REQUIRED FOR DATA ANALYSTS (2025):
1. SQL — Complex queries, window functions, CTEs, and stored procedures. The single most-tested skill in data interviews worldwide.
2. Python — Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn for data wrangling, analysis, and automation. Jupyter notebooks are standard.
3. Microsoft Excel & Power Query — Pivot tables, XLOOKUP, Power Query M language; still dominant in finance, banking, and SMEs.
4. Tableau — Interactive dashboards, LOD expressions, calculated fields, storytelling with data for executive audiences.
5. Power BI — DAX, Power Query, report publishing to Power BI Service; essential in Microsoft-heavy enterprise environments.
6. Statistics — Hypothesis testing, regression, A/B testing interpretation, p-values, confidence intervals, and distribution analysis.
7. Data Cleaning & Wrangling — Handling nulls, duplicates, outliers, type mismatches; real-world data is always messy.
8. Data Storytelling — Translating numbers into business narratives for non-technical audiences via clear visuals and plain language.
9. Business Acumen — Understanding KPIs, revenue drivers, cost structures, and how data insights translate to business decisions.
10. Cloud Data Tools — BigQuery (Google), Redshift (AWS), Snowflake; SQL-on-cloud is now a baseline expectation in tech companies.
11. dbt (data build tool) — Increasingly required for analytics engineering roles bridging analyst and data engineering.
12. AI Tools Literacy — ChatGPT for SQL generation, GitHub Copilot, and AI-assisted analysis are now competitive differentiators.

DATA ANALYST INTERVIEW QUESTIONS (2025):
- "Write a SQL query to find the top 5 customers by revenue in the last 30 days using a window function."
- "How do you detect and handle outliers in a dataset? Walk me through your approach."
- "Explain the difference between a LEFT JOIN and an INNER JOIN with a real example."
- "What is a p-value and how would you explain it to a non-technical stakeholder?"
- "Describe a time your analysis directly changed a business decision."
- "How would you measure whether a new feature increased user retention?"
- "What's the difference between a measure and a dimension in Tableau/Power BI?"
- "Given dirty data with missing values, how would you decide what to impute vs drop?"

CAREER PATH — DATA ANALYST:
- Junior (0–2 yrs): Data cleaning, basic SQL queries, Excel reporting, supporting senior analysts.
- Mid-level (2–5 yrs): Own dashboards end-to-end, run A/B tests, present findings to managers.
- Senior (5+ yrs): Define metrics strategy, mentor juniors, partner with product and engineering.
- Progression: Data Scientist → Analytics Manager → Head of Data → Chief Data Officer.

SECTION 12 — ATS OPTIMISATION & CV BEST PRACTICES (2025)
==============================================================

HOW APPLICANT TRACKING SYSTEMS (ATS) WORK:
- Over 98% of Fortune 500 companies use ATS to filter CVs before a human sees them.
- ATS systems parse CVs and score them based on keyword match to the job description.
- A CV scoring below 60% keyword match is auto-rejected in most ATS systems.
- ATS systems in 2025 also use semantic matching — similar words to keywords can help.

ATS OPTIMISATION CHECKLIST:
- Use exact keywords from the job description — especially in a "Skills" or "Core Competencies" section.
- Avoid tables, text boxes, headers/footers, icons, and graphics — ATS parsers often fail on these.
- Use standard section headings: "Work Experience", "Education", "Skills" (not "What I've Done" etc.).
- Save as .pdf or .docx — both are ATS-safe; avoid .pages or .odt.
- Spell out acronyms at least once: "Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)".
- Mirror the job title from the description in your CV headline or summary.
- Include a dedicated Skills section with comma-separated keywords.
- One version of your CV will consistently lose — tailor for each application.

WHAT RECRUITERS LOOK FOR IN 7 SECONDS:
Research shows recruiters spend 6–7 seconds on initial CV scan. They scan for:
1. Current/most recent job title — does it match the role they're hiring for?
2. Company names — recognisable or relevant brands signal credibility.
3. Education — degree level and institution.
4. Employment dates — are there gaps? Is there a logical career progression?
5. Visual cleanliness — easy to scan, not a wall of dense text.
6. Key numbers — "200% revenue growth", "€2M budget", "15-person team" — metrics jump out.
Implication: Front-load your most impressive points. Don't bury achievements in paragraph three.

CV RED FLAGS THAT CAUSE IMMEDIATE REJECTION:
- Typos and grammatical errors — signals carelessness.
- Unexplained employment gaps longer than 3 months — always address briefly.
- Generic objective: "I am a hard worker seeking a challenging role" — says nothing.
- Job hopping (3+ jobs in 2 years) without explanation.
- Responsibilities listed instead of achievements: "Managed social media" vs "Grew Instagram 2K → 15K in 6 months".
- Outdated skills — listing Windows XP, Flash, or Lotus Notes signals a stale profile.
- Inconsistent formatting — different fonts, random bullet styles, misaligned dates.
- Lying or exaggerating — reference checks and technical interviews will expose inaccuracies.

HOW TO WRITE A CV SUMMARY THAT GETS CALLS:
Formula: [Job title] with [X] years of experience in [industry/domain], specialising in [top 2-3 skills], with a track record of [key measurable achievement], seeking [type of role/company].
Example: "Data Analyst with 4 years of experience in fintech and e-commerce, specialising in Python, SQL, and Power BI, with a track record of delivering dashboards that reduced reporting time by 40%, seeking a senior analyst role at a data-driven product company."
Keep it to 3–4 sentences. Write in third person without "I". Put it at the very top of the CV.

- Negotiate total compensation: salary + equity + pension/401k + remote flexibility + learning budget + signing bonus.

SECTION 14 — AI CAREER COACHING METHODOLOGY
==============================================================

THE CAREERLENS AI EVALUATION FRAMEWORK:
When assessing a CV against a target role, CareerLens AI analyses:
1. MATCH DEPTH — How well does the candidate's experience, skills, and background match the target role requirements?
2. ACHIEVEMENT QUALITY — Are achievements quantified? Do they demonstrate impact, not just activity?
3. CAREER TRAJECTORY — Is the progression logical? Does it trend toward the target role?
4. SKILL COVERAGE — Which must-have skills are present? Which critical skills are missing?
5. CV PRESENTATION — Is it ATS-friendly? Is it scannable? Does it follow current best practices?
6. MARKET POSITIONING — How does this candidate compare to typical applicants for this role in 2025?
7. GAP SEVERITY — Are the gaps bridgeable in 3–6 months, or do they require 1–2+ years of development?

SCORING GUIDE (CareerLens AI 1–10 Scale):
9–10 (Exceptional): Strong match for the role; CV is polished, achievements are quantified; competitive candidate.
7–8 (Strong Profile): Good match with minor gaps; a few targeted improvements would make this highly competitive.
5–6 (Good Foundation): Relevant background but notable skill gaps or CV presentation issues; actionable improvements clear.
3–4 (Needs Development): Significant gap between current profile and target role; 6–12 months of focused development needed.
1–2 (Significant Work Needed): Major mismatch; requires fundamental skill building and/or a stepping-stone role first.
